The proposal
1   Summary
negustolonow is a house in Detroit that makes up worlds, makes the objects that belong in them, and
teaches other people to do the same. It is the brand over four things that form one loop: a website you
walk through instead of scroll, real objects modelled from that world, a workshop curriculum, and a public
record for each made thing. That record is a token: an entry on a public ledger that one account holds
and can pass to another, usually called an NFT. This document proposes the next stage: print the first
pieces, issue their records, open the worlds to play in real rooms, and establish a suitable legal entity to hold
the common treasury.



The thesis: Value first. Token second. A token is worth exactly what it is the record of, so we issue one
only for something already worth having without it. The house has its own word for this. In Ossel, the first
world it made up, every made thing arrives with a docket: a small note of what was made and what was
done while making it. Nobody wants a docket without a chair. The token is the docket.

Whose intelligence. Your kid already has an AI. The question is whose. This proposal's answer is grown
on the block: the SESHAT Seed Project, as introduced by Uri Heru House, in conversation, is exploring a
computational intelligence that a neighbourhood stewards itself. negustolonow would feed it by hand,
only with work its players have chosen to have credited, and put it to work at a peace table, where two
rooms that disagree make something together instead of making speeches (section 16). Nothing of it
exists yet.

2    The problem
Three kinds of digital thing lost their value in the same decade, for the same reason: nothing stood
behind them.

    1. The game you bought. It is a licence. It cannot be lent, resold or kept when the store closes. Robot
       Cache, a store built on resale, closed in May 2026, and its users reportedly lost their games.
 2. The NFT you bought. It was a receipt for an image anyone could copy. One study in September 2023
    found 69,795 of 73,257 NFT collections at a market value of zero; it is one study, and it counted
    collections. Market sales in 2025 were $5.63 billion, about 37% down on 2024.
3. The picture you generated. It belongs to nobody, and it looks like it.

The usual NFT pitch started with the token and went looking for a reason to want it. We reverse the order.
A token is issued only for a thing that is wanted without it: an object you can hold, a claim on one print of
a design, a game you can play, an item that matters inside a game, or what you kept from an evening in a
made-up place. The pattern has a survivor: Courtyard, whose tokens stand for graded trading cards held
in a vault, raised a $30 million Series A in July 2025, by its own account.

    State today: The thesis is untested against our own goods. Nothing has been sold or
    issued.

Physical to digital. The Dummy figure is an open, 3D-printable action figure with a 26-step assembly;
its base design, Dummy 13, is by soozafone under the CC BY 4.0 licence. A student builds one, then
animates it in the stop-motion programme "A Box of Kittens", whose production is "8 Minutes". The
curriculum is "Make Your Own, Kid!", taught as STEAM+E+D, and its arc is Action Figure to Action Taker.

Digital to digital. The house is a website you walk through. Ossel is a 900-kilometre seawall country
where the machines do all the work and hold no authority; Mercy Room, the second world, is one room
and four garments. There is a pocket on every page: each thing a visitor passes gets one of three
answers, Keep it, Not yet, or Leave it. The visit ends on the step, where a figure with its back turned is
drawn only from what the visitor kept, with a printable docket ending in the word LOOK.

Digital to physical. Backroom is the TechniBears portal at backroom.technibears.com. Its Arena (the
Dummy 13 Arena) is a 3D parts bench: a user picks a figure, gives each of its thirteen parts a skill, and
exports a file a printer can make. What we sell on this crossing is a print code: a token that stands for
one print of a closed design, made by us. Its holder can keep it, trade it, or redeem it for the piece, and
never receives the design file.

Play. The worlds become games played in real rooms. A player opts in, in person, at a key room: a room
whose keeper holds a gamemaster key, a signing key to which the house account gives a narrow set of
powers. Players carry made pieces, and a chip inside a piece lets it prove which piece it is. People
already pay for this: one platform reports more than $50 million paid to about 5,000 gamemasters since
2019, by its own claim. The product is a format licensed to rooms that already exist, not a venue we build.

How quadratic funding would work. Quadratic funding shares out a pool of matching money between
pieces of work. Each one's match grows with the number of people who gave to it, not only with the
amount: the match is proportional to the square of the sum of the square roots of the gifts. So a hundred
gifts of one dollar draw far more matching than one gift of a hundred dollars, and many small givers
count for more than one large one. The rules of each round and the size of its matching pool will be
published before the round opens. The method's known weak point is telling one person from many,
because one giver can split a gift across made-up names. So a giver who wants their gift to count
toward matching opts in, in person, at a key room.

   State today: The address exists and has received funds before. No round has been run. No
   matching pool exists. No rules are published.

Product requirements
7    The people it is for
The visitor wants an evening somewhere else, and gets a house to walk through with no account, feed or
score, and a figure and a docket to keep.

The student wants to make a thing and say why it matters, and gets the path from concept sketch to
final pitch, a finished piece, and a record under their own name.

The player wants a game with real objects and other people in a room, and gets a table, a card, a figure,
and items that outlast the session.

The keeper, or gamemaster, runs a key room and wants a format that fills it weekly. They get a licence to
run it, a key with narrow powers, and pay for their time.

The maker wants to be paid for a piece, and again if it is resold. They get a capped edition, a record that
travels with the piece, and a collection fee that marketplaces are encouraged, not forced, to pay.

The funder wants to know exactly what their money buys, and gets a named classification, a public
record of every vote and payment, and state lines that say what exists.

    State today: Visitors and students exist. No player, keeper or funder has been admitted.




8    Principles that bind every product
    1. Value first. No token is issued for anything not already worth having without it.
 2. Say what exists. Every surface carries a state line. "None printed" stays until a piece is printed.
3. The school is open and the house is closed. The Dummy figure and the curriculum can be taken
   apart by anyone, and students own their files. The house's design files stay with us; the made piece is
   what is sold.
4. Consent is an object you hold. A card held out in the room means yes. A card put away means not
   now.




NEGUSTOLONOW                                                                                              page 12
5. Nothing personal on chain. Not a name, not an age, and not a hash of either. A hash, also called a
   fingerprint, is a short code computed from a text.
 6. No reward that is a coin. Nothing depends on a token's price.
    7. No paid entry into anything decided by chance. Items are awarded for what a player did.
8. Children are students, not labour. Their work is credited to them and held for them. Nothing is paid.

     State today: Principles 1 to 3 are practised on the live site. The rest are written rules
     for products not yet built.

16    The SESHAT Seed, and the peace table
Whose? The neighbourhood's. The SESHAT Seed Project, as introduced by Uri Heru House, in
conversation, is exploring an intelligence that a community grows and keeps for itself. Not rented from a
company far away. Grown on the block. negustolonow is where a Seed would be fed, and where it would
be put to work: at a table between two rooms that disagree. Nothing of it exists yet. This section sets the
rules it would keep from the first day.



What the Seed is
In the project's own words, it is "a locally stewarded computational intelligence that can learn, remember,
and grow in relationship with the people and communities it serves". It is called a Seed because it does
not arrive finished: the project is creating, again in its words, "a foundation from which one can grow", and
the seed carries seven principles: stewardship, trust, respect, curiosity, shared responsibility, resilience
and regeneration. A finished thing can be rented. A seed has to be planted somewhere, by someone.

Prompt Craft puts one pair of hands on the tool (section 18). CH313 gives the neighbourhood its own
network (section 20). The Seed is what the block would grow on it, and it answers to the block. The
project's rule is the mesh's own: "Local does not mean isolated." A community keeps its own knowledge
and its own infrastructure, and stays on speaking terms with everyone else.



What it is fed
Data as labour is the Seed's diet, and the card is the switch. What players deliberately make and decide
in the build space, cards out, is credited to each of them (section 15). Pooled, that record is the only
thing a Seed may learn from, and only once a recorded vote of the company, cast by the players
concerned (section 30), has licensed that use. Fed by hand. Never by watching.

The peace table
The assembly already sends rooms to negotiate a real question (section 13). The peace table carries it
one step further, and it is a peace-building endeavour. Each room grows its own Seed. When two rooms
that disagree meet, they do not start with speeches. They start with something to make.

   On the table: objects, dockets, models, moves in a game, proposals.
   On the books: a trade, shared budget, or proposed matching round only if funded.

The Seeds carry and translate. Each brings what its block knows, in a shape the other side can pick up.
People decide and ratify: no Seed signs for anyone, and nothing is settled until named humans in both
rooms ratify it, as at the assembly (GM-6). Settle one real question, and the words can wait.


   Skip the speeches. Make something together.




NEGUSTOLONOW                                                                                         page 20
The divide is always local and always concrete: two rooms, two blocks, two sides of a street. It is bridged
by making something together, not by agreeing on words first. The evidence for role-play and Model
United Nations learning is real but modest, so this is promising and not proven, and no outcome is
claimed.



A nod to Block by Block
The peace table is played on a real place. That is a nod to the UN-Habitat Minecraft programme, Block by
Block, in which residents rebuild a real public space in Minecraft, redesign it together, and put it in front
of the officials who decide. We already use its budgeting method (section 5). Here we borrow two more
of its moves, as an experiment:

 1. The board is the real street, in blocks. A street, a lot or a square, modelled so both rooms can walk
    through it. Both sides build on it: proposals, trades and a shared budget, laid on the model where
    both can see.
 2. The result goes on the desk of whoever decides. A landlord, a block club, a city department.

Put the street on the table. The model is the board, and the real street is what is being negotiated.

We take its warning too. Block by Block warns that participatory design without a serious commitment to
build can raise false hopes and damage future civic participation. In our words: we don't hand out pencils
for streets nobody means to build. As in section 5, no partnership or endorsement is implied. The
programme's core funding was reportedly cut in 2025, so we will confirm its current form before
anything is built on it.

21    Accounts and permissions
How an account is controlled. A key is a pair: a private half that signs, and a public half anyone can
check a signature against. A Vaulta account has named permissions, each a threshold plus a list of
weighted keys. A multisig, short for multi-signature, is a permission that needs more than one key. The
action updateauth changes a permission. The action linkauth binds a permission to one specific
action, so that it can do that and nothing else.

Facts verified on chain on 1 October 2026:

     negustolonow was created on 29 December 2018. No contract is deployed on it. Its owner and
     active permissions each have one key.

     On 27 September 2026 it gained a permission named wilibansqnfa under active , with nothing
     linked to it.
     negustolo.gm was created on 25 September 2026 by the account gm . Its active key is the same
     public key as wilibansqnfa .
     negustolonow has authored no AtomicAssets collection.

24   A session, end to end (Proposed)
 1. Opt-in. On a first visit to a key room the player opts in, in front of the keeper. Their wallet shows the
    player agreement as a Ricardian clause, the plain-language term attached to the action being signed
    (section 29), and the player signs with their own key.
2. Card. The room's gamemaster permission signs the issue of a card.
3. Opening. The gamemaster permission signs the opening of a session at its own room.
4. "I was there." The player puts the card out and signs a presence message with their own key, over the
   session's identifier and a fresh number.
5. "They were there." The keeper countersigns with the gamemaster permission.
6. Play. Each event is signed by the player who made it. A tap is signed by the chip in the piece.
 7. Record. Each player's part of the session record is encrypted on their own device with their own data
    key, then stored off the chain.
8. Docket. The keeper closes the session and signs the docket: what was made and done, by account
   and by piece, with no personal data. A fingerprint of the docket goes on chain.

The two-signature rule. A presence counts only with both signatures, the player's and the room's.
Neither can create attendance alone.

With the internet down. The mesh is transport, not security: its channel encryption uses a shared key
with no authentication, so anyone with that key can pose as anyone. So every event and presence is a
signed message, which fits in one radio packet. The room's device checks each signature against the
player's public key, drops anything else, and keeps the messages in order. When a connection returns,
the keeper submits them, and the contract checks the same signatures before entering the docket. Until
then the session is provisional on the record.

  State today: Proposed. No action exists and no session has run.

    whoever made them: the player's key for their own work, the room's gamemaster key for the docket. It
    holds no key of its own and signs nothing. No camera, microphone or biometric reading exists for it to
    read.
 6. Erasure is of records, not of learning. Deleting a player's data key erases their records. It does not
    prove that anything a Seed already learned from them is forgotten, and nothing here promises that it
    is. How a Seed holds what it learns is an open design question, to be settled before any Seed is fed.

  State today: Rules written. No record store, no data key, no data collected. No Seed fed.

PART D / COMPANY ARCHITECTURE




28 The wrapper and the choice
Negustolonow is a creative house and workshop programme, not a real-estate company. Its proposed legal wrapper would
hold the common treasury, make contracts, employ or pay makers, licence the game to rooms, and administer rights that
participants deliberately grant. This is an adaptation of the Red Door Digital DAO architecture, not a claim that the two
organisations are the same.
The Red Door route, translated
Option 1 - Wyoming DAO LLC, operating in Michigan. The Red Door blueprint calls for a Wyoming DAO LLC and, when its
Detroit activities constitute transacting business, a Michigan Certificate of Authority for a foreign LLC. Wyoming has a
DAO-specific statute (W.S. 17-31), with required naming, registered agent, public smart-contract identifiers where
applicable, and notice of possible restrictions on duties and transfers. A member-managed structure is the proposed
starting point because no automated governance contract exists.

Option 2 - Michigan LLC governed by a DAO-style agreement. This was the route in the previous version. It avoids
maintaining a Wyoming entity plus Michigan foreign qualification. It can write member voting, multisignature treasury
approvals and online ratification into its operating agreement, but it must not call itself a Wyoming statutory DAO LLC.
A decision with consequences
The two routes cannot be blended by rhetoric. A Wyoming registration is not a Michigan-organised issuer. In particular,
Michigan MILE intrastate crowdfunding requires an issuer organised under Michigan law, among other conditions. If a
Michigan-only securities offering matters, that is a material reason to prefer or separately examine the Michigan route. A
securities attorney must resolve the structure before creating Class B interests.

Wyoming-specific operating duties matter: the statutory notice on member rights, article/smart-contract amendments, the
registered agent and annual filings, and dissolution after one year without approved proposals or action. The agreement
should set a real governance cadence, not a sham heartbeat.

  State today: No new company has been formed, no route approved by counsel, and no members admitted. TechniBears LLC is
  an existing, separate Michigan company.

29 The contract stack
Red Door supplies the organising pattern: (1) an off-chain legal contract, (2) on-chain execution, and (3) a human-readable
Ricardian bridge. The three must be versioned and connected, but no signature technique alone guarantees judicial
enforceability.

 Legal master               Articles of organisation and operating agreement identify the entity, members, class rights, decision
                            thresholds, delegated powers, exit rules, conflict policies, IP and data licences, bank signers and dispute
                            forum. They contain the actual obligations.

 Execution record           Vaulta account permissions, timestamped proposal records, signatures and treasury transactions
                            document execution. Start with narrowly scoped permissions and manual multisignature approvals; a
                            custom contract comes only after a security review.

 Ricardian bridge           Publish stable text for each action and terms version. Include a document ID, jurisdiction, rights
                            conferred, party, signature method, effective time, amendment rule and hash. Verify how the actual
                            wallet renders it; display is not automatic in every client.


The hierarchy is a legal question
The earlier line, "where paper and chain disagree, paper governs," is too absolute for a Wyoming DAO LLC. Wyoming law
gives particular priority to articles and smart contracts in defined conflicts (W.S. 17-31-115). The operating agreement
must be drafted to reconcile those rules. The controlling hierarchy and emergency correction process are an attorney
deliverable, not an application default.
The voice and the bookplate
Red Door proposes reading the agreement aloud, retaining a recording, and signing a dated bookplate. Keep that ceremony
as a record of deliberation and stewardship. It does not by itself transfer membership, title, or a legal share. Admission
requires the actual articles/agreement, member register, signatures, and any applicable issuance or securities
documentation. Keep recordings off-chain, with explicit consent and retention rules; do not record minors.

  or legally vetted.

Appendix I - Offline and edge architecture
The Red Door operational sketch names PaperMC/Minecraft, Nextcloud, RaspAP, Meshtastic, Raspberry Pi, Nvidia Jetson,
Canon PRISMAsync and OrcaSlicer. These technologies solve different problems. Treat them as a modular experiment, not
one ready-made platform.

 LAN and portal              RaspAP may manage a local wireless access point; an HTTPS service/portal must be built and hosted
                             separately. Nextcloud is a file and collaboration platform, not the captive portal, WordPress, or public
                             tunnel itself.

 Community mesh              Meshtastic carries low-bitrate, signed status/events and short messages. It does not carry Minecraft
                             sessions or full internet access. Do not claim one whole authenticated event fits a packet until
                             byte-budget tests pass.

 Game and files              PaperMC requires a properly sized host and compatible Java version. Nextcloud needs supported
                             deployment, storage, updates and backups. A public tunnel works only when an upstream internet
                             connection exists.

 Production                  A shop-controlled queue API or bridge must mediate Canon PRISMAsync and 3D printers. OrcaSlicer
                             exports toolpaths; it is not a validated multi-printer scheduling service by itself.

 Local AI                    The standard Raspberry Pi AI HAT+ does not run LLMs; the HAT+ 2 or suitably sized Jetson/other host
                             can be evaluated for on-device models. A Pi 5 with 1 GB is a thin controller, not the entire stack.


Required pilot tests
   Print-shop device inventory, API/licence permissions, protected operator controls and a manual override.
   No-internet drill with RaspAP/Meshtastic; distinguish local-Wi-Fi availability from mesh reachability.
   Measured memory/load, thermal and model-licence budgets before naming hardware as a production specification.

  State today: This is a design correction to the Red Door workshop vision, not a claim the stack has been installed.

Appendix II - Rights and provenance
Physical goods + tokens
A printed part, its on-chain record and its legal rights are three separate things. Publish the object specification,
edition/piece number, delivery/refund warranty, licensed design use and transfer conditions. A token does not force
off-chain delivery or continued hosting. Signing NFC proves a response from one chip key, not the unaltered identity of
the piece.
Accounts and permissions
A restricted Vaulta permission is not sufficient by itself to confine a gamemaster to a room. Each game action must check
caller account, permission, venue, nonce and role inside the executing contract. Hardware-backed keys, approvals,
revocation, testnet rehearsal and audit trails are acceptance criteria. Never equate token ownership with the actual holder
of a physical object unless a documented handover links them.
Consent and children
The player card signifies a physical choice only when a documented off-chain process defines that choice. Guardian
verification, withdrawal and purpose-specific licensing are needed. A public vote by the company does not replace a
creator's consent to train on their work. Encrypt session records by default, implement backup retention periods and avoid
the misleading promise that deleting one key removes every copy or untrains a model.
